LESSON 01 / 16
Pretraining objectives and next-token prediction
The exact supervision signal, causal information flow, and why next-token learning yields reusable representations.


A Interviewer questions
  1. Derive the causal language-model likelihood for a sequence of tokens.
  2. Why does teacher forcing not leak future information in causal pretraining?
  3. Why is next-token accuracy not sufficient to guarantee factuality or instruction following?
B Deep technical explanation
1 · Mechanism & data flow
A decoder-only model sees token IDs x₀…xₜ₋₁. Embeddings and position mechanisms feed a stack of causal self-attention and MLP blocks; the hidden state at each position maps to vocabulary logits. During training, the same forward pass predicts all shifted targets in parallel, since attention masks prohibit accessing later positions. During generation, sampling extends the prefix one token at a time. Teacher forcing provides the real prefix rather than feeding the model's own generated tokens; this creates a training–inference exposure gap. Language modeling induces syntactic, semantic and some world-pattern representations because compressed predictions benefit from latent regularities, but its objective does not directly optimize truth, calibration or helpfulness.
2 · Objective & mathematics
pθ(x₁:T) = ∏_{t=1}^T pθ(x_t | x_<t);   L_NLL = − Σ_t m_t log pθ(x_t | x_<t) / Σ_t m_t
Here mₜ is a loss mask (0 for padding or excluded positions). The final-position logits cannot see its future target; shifted labels align position t−1 to xₜ.
3 · Architecture & alternatives
Compared with masked language modeling, autoregression is naturally suited to left-to-right generation. Prefix language modeling, fill-in-the-middle infilling and multimodal pretraining modify what counts as visible context; they require explicit masks/objectives.
4 · Implementation detail
Build input_ids = tokens[:-1] and labels = tokens[1:] for a simple model; production causal LM interfaces may perform the shift internally. Check tokenization, EOS handling, padding and attention masks; never shift twice. Compute CE on valid labels only.
5 · Compute / latency / memory / cost
At sequence length S, full attention materialization is O(S²) elements per head while compute scales with attention FLOPs plus dense projections. Pretraining throughput is usually reported as training tokens/s, hardware utilization, and loss per token; gradient accumulation changes effective batch but not data diversity.
6 · Failure modes & debugging
Duplicate-heavy corpora may improve training loss while harming generalization; teacher forcing masks exposure errors. Data contamination can make benchmarks invalid. High next-token confidence is not evidence of factual grounding.


C Interview-ready spoken answer
“A decoder-only LLM estimates a conditional distribution for each next token. During pretraining we use shifted targets and a causal mask, so all token losses are computed in parallel without future leakage. We minimize masked cross-entropy with backpropagation. This develops transferable language representations, but factuality and user alignment need separate data, objectives and evaluations.”


D Follow-up pressure test
  1. How would you prove the causal mask is effective? Discuss prefix-invariance under suffix changes.
  2. If teacher forcing is parallel, why is generation sequential? Distinguish training vs autoregressive decode.
  3. What happens if you accidentally score pad or prompt tokens in the loss?
E Production scenario — investigate, mitigate, validate
Constraint & symptom: A model shows excellent train perplexity but poor factual QA on an internal holdout.
Instrument: Separate train/validation NLL by domain, exact/near-duplicate rates, QA accuracy and calibration with dated holdout provenance.
Test competing hypotheses: Compare train–eval overlap, label shift, domain imbalance and generation-time exposure errors with targeted ablations.
Mitigate & trade off: Deduplicate and rebalance licensed sources; preserve untouched holdout; add task-specific SFT or retrieval if requirements demand current evidence.
Acceptance gate: Require improvement on leakage-controlled factual QA without degradation on a general language holdout; define thresholds before retraining.
F Interview takeaways
  • Causal masking—not label secrecy—prevents future information leakage.
  • Shift labels exactly once; ignore pads deliberately.
  • Likelihood is a training surrogate, not proof of truth or helpfulness.


LESSON 02 / 16
Cross-entropy loss: derivation and gradients
Compute softmax CE, derive its stable gradient, and diagnose training instability.


A Interviewer questions
  1. Derive ∂L/∂z_j for one target from the softmax and negative log-likelihood.
  2. Why use logsumexp instead of computing log(softmax(logits)) naïvely?
  3. What is the effect of label smoothing or token masking on gradients?
B Deep technical explanation
1 · Mechanism & data flow
Given vocabulary logits z and target class y, the model converts logits into probabilities p with softmax. Loss is the negative log-probability of the correct next token. Differentiation gives a clean gradient: each logit receives its predicted probability, minus one for the target logit. This pushes the correct logit upward and others downward, distributed by their current mass. With masked sequences, normalization should use the number of valid target tokens—not the padded rectangular tensor size. Label smoothing changes the one-hot target into a mixture and can soften overconfidence, but may hurt exact-token preference for constrained output. Numerical overflow/underflow is avoided by subtracting the maximum logit (or calling fused cross-entropy).
2 · Objective & mathematics
p_j = exp(z_j − z_max) / Σ_k exp(z_k − z_max)
L = −log p_y = log(Σ_k exp z_k) − z_y;   ∂L/∂z_j = p_j − 1[j=y]
For soft targets q: ∂L/∂zⱼ = pⱼ−qⱼ. With a mean reduction over M valid positions, divide aggregated gradients by M.
3 · Architecture & alternatives
Cross-entropy fits classification over a known vocabulary. Sequence-level ranking, preferences and reward are different optimization objects; CE alone ignores whether a long completion is helpful or whether two answers differ in utility.
4 · Implementation detail
Use torch.nn.functional.cross_entropy(logits.reshape(-1,V), labels.reshape(-1), ignore_index=-100). Verify shift and that masking does not silently reduce to zero labels. Use float32 for unstable reductions when appropriate.
5 · Compute / latency / memory / cost
Softmax projection is O(BSV) for logits/CE; very large V consumes activation memory. Mixed-precision training needs stable reductions and monitored gradient norms, loss spikes and nonfinite counts.
6 · Failure modes & debugging
Common traps: softmax twice, logits/label mismatch, target out of range, loss reduction including pad positions, all-ignored labels yielding NaN under some paths, or incorrect interpretation of negative log-likelihood as “percent wrong.”


C Interview-ready spoken answer
“Cross-entropy is the negative log-likelihood of the target token. Softmax turns logits into probabilities, and the gradient with respect to a logit is p minus its one-hot target. That is why CE is efficient to optimize end-to-end. I use numerically stable fused CE, verify the label shift and masks, and monitor gradient norms rather than relying on loss alone.”


D Follow-up pressure test
  1. Compute CE and the gradient by hand for logits [2, 1, 0] and target index 1.
  2. Why can average CE improve while rare-domain accuracy falls?
  3. What is the failure mode of adding softmax before a CE function that expects logits?
E Production scenario — investigate, mitigate, validate
Constraint & symptom: SFT loss becomes NaN after increasing the maximum sequence length.
Instrument: Log histogram of logits and gradient norms, count nonfinite tensors, inspect ignored-label counts and optimizer scales by step.
Test competing hypotheses: Compare fused CE with manual softmax, detect mixed-precision overflow, and run a fixed batch in float32.
Mitigate & trade off: Use stable CE, gradient clipping after unscale, valid-token averaging and rollback checkpoints; do not hide recurring NaNs by skipping indefinitely.
Acceptance gate: No nonfinite gradients over an agreed stress window; evaluate unchanged golden outputs and throughput impact.
F Interview takeaways
  • CE gradient: p−one-hot.
  • Calculate logsumexp stably; trust raw logits, not pre-softmaxed inputs.
  • Inspect valid-label masks and reduction semantics.


LESSON 03 / 16
Backpropagation and optimization in Transformers
Trace the gradients through attention, residual streams and distributed optimizer state.


A Interviewer questions
  1. Explain the reverse-mode chain rule from the loss back through a Transformer block.
  2. Compare AdamW vs SGD for large language-model pretraining.
  3. How would you detect exploding gradients without masking the underlying cause?
B Deep technical explanation
1 · Mechanism & data flow
Forward: embeddings → repeated norm/attention/residual/MLP → final projection → CE. Backward uses reverse-mode autodiff to multiply local Jacobian-vector products through every differentiable operation. For residual y=x+f(x), ∂L/∂x includes a direct identity path in addition to f’s Jacobian, improving gradient flow. Gradient accumulation sums/averages microbatch contributions before an optimizer step. AdamW tracks exponential moments of gradients and squared gradients and applies decoupled weight decay. Mixed precision, loss scaling, activation checkpointing, fused kernels, distributed sharding and global gradient clipping alter resource use without changing the desired mathematical objective when implemented correctly.
2 · Objective & mathematics
∂L/∂x = (I + J_f(x))ᵀ (∂L/∂y)   when y=x+f(x)
m_t=β₁m_{t−1}+(1−β₁)g_t; v_t=β₂v_{t−1}+(1−β₂)g_t²
θ_{t+1} = θ_t − η m̂_t/(√v̂_t+ε) − ηλθ_t  (AdamW-style)
3 · Architecture & alternatives
AdamW often offers fast practical optimization but requires optimizer moments; SGD reduces state but can be harder to tune. Gradient checkpointing saves activation memory but recomputes blocks. FSDP/ZeRO shard states with communication overhead.
4 · Implementation detail
Use optimizer.zero_grad(set_to_none=True), loss.backward(), clip_grad_norm_(), optimizer.step(); with AMP unscale before clipping. Accumulated loss must be weighted by real token count if microbatches vary in valid tokens.
5 · Compute / latency / memory / cost
Adam moments typically occupy ~8 bytes/parameter in FP32 (plus other weights/grads/master weights as applicable). Activation memory grows with microbatch, sequence length and layers; checkpointing trades compute for memory.
6 · Failure modes & debugging
Watch unused parameters, gradient desynchronization in distributed training, all-reduce order mismatches, precision overflow and incorrect scheduler steps per microbatch.


C Interview-ready spoken answer
“Backprop computes loss gradients through the attention and MLP stacks using chain rule, while residual paths give a direct gradient route. AdamW combines first- and second-moment updates with decoupled weight decay. In production I separate math correctness from optimization tactics such as AMP, accumulation, sharding and checkpointing, and track norm spikes, skipped steps and valid-token normalization.”


D Follow-up pressure test
  1. Explain a gradient-accumulation bug with variable-length microbatches.
  2. Which memory terms remain after activation checkpointing?
  3. If gradient norms spike only on one data shard, where do you investigate first?
E Production scenario — investigate, mitigate, validate
Constraint & symptom: A 32-GPU run diverges while a 1-GPU reproduction trains.
Instrument: Track rank-wise nonfinite counts, per-rank token counts, loss scales, gradient norm before/after clipping and collective errors.
Test competing hypotheses: Test accumulation weighting, distributed masks, mixed precision and shard-specific poisoned data separately.
Mitigate & trade off: Reproduce with a deterministic microbatch, fix reductions and clipping order, then resume from validated checkpoint.
Acceptance gate: All ranks agree on reduced loss and gradient summaries on a fixed distributed fixture; no divergence on a replayed suspect shard.
F Interview takeaways
  • Residuals improve, but do not guarantee, gradient propagation.
  • Optimizer state can exceed raw weight memory.
  • Correctly average by valid tokens across accumulation and replicas.


LESSON 04 / 16
Training data quality, contamination and memorization
Build trustworthy datasets and distinguish robust learning from benchmark leakage.


A Interviewer questions
  1. How can exact and near-duplicate data distort training and evaluation?
  2. How would you test memorization without collecting sensitive data unnecessarily?
  3. How do you govern licensing, provenance and deletion requests across checkpoints?
B Deep technical explanation
1 · Mechanism & data flow
A modern pipeline ingests permitted sources, records provenance, removes malformed and nonlinguistic noise, deduplicates exact/near-duplicates, filters sensitive and unsafe content as required, performs quality/difficulty balancing, splits evaluation before optimization and creates immutable dataset versions. MinHash/LSH can approximate near-duplicate detection; hashes alone miss paraphrases and template variants. Model memorization is more likely for repeated, rare, high-salience examples, but exposure depends on training and decoding. Benchmark contamination includes overlap with answers, not merely question exact matches. Governance requires retention policy, collection license, document-level lineage and incident response; simply deleting a file does not reliably remove effects from already-trained weights.
2 · Objective & mathematics
dup_rate ≈ (# sampled documents flagged duplicate) / (# sampled documents inspected)
This is an operational dataset statistic, not a memorization-probability estimator. For a canary exposure test, measure extraction under a documented attack budget against a matched random baseline.
3 · Architecture & alternatives
Heuristic filtering is inexpensive but may bias language or writing styles. Model-scored filtering adapts to content but inherits scorer biases. Domain oversampling may help target quality yet overfit repeated facts.
4 · Implementation detail
Implement content hashes, normalized text similarity, provenance IDs and immutable train/eval manifests; maintain an isolation buffer around evaluations. Restrict sensitive-text access and use minimal logged samples.
5 · Compute / latency / memory / cost
Deduplication costs CPU/index storage but can reduce wasted token budget. Data quality can dominate marginal model-scale improvements. Evaluate per-source/language coverage and downstream tasks, not a single aggregate quality score.
6 · Failure modes & debugging
Never equate removal from future training with guaranteed machine unlearning. A suspicious evaluation win requires overlap audit before interpreting the score. Filtering can unintentionally remove minority-language examples.


C Interview-ready spoken answer
“I treat data as versioned model input with provenance, licensing and leakage controls. I deduplicate exact and near-duplicate texts, isolate holdouts before training, and audit overlap with benchmark answers. I evaluate by domain and memorization probes, because lower training loss can reflect repetition, and deletion from a dataset is not the same as removing learned information from existing weights.”


D Follow-up pressure test
  1. How can your benchmark be contaminated without exact copied prompts?
  2. How would you balance multilingual coverage when a filter favors English?
  3. What evidence would you provide for a privacy incident involving model memorization?
E Production scenario — investigate, mitigate, validate
Constraint & symptom: A newly trained model jumps in coding benchmark scores but user tests do not improve.
Instrument: Compare overlap fingerprints, repository/version provenance, post-training updates and private unseen code tests.
Test competing hypotheses: Investigate benchmark solution leakage, prompt template overlap and outdated public suite versions.
Mitigate & trade off: Quarantine the suspect result; rebuild holdouts; retrain/evaluate with versioned excluded sources and verified licenses.
Acceptance gate: Gains survive temporally separated, unseen problems with no detected training overlap at the chosen similarity threshold.
F Interview takeaways
  • Data curation is optimization, not just cleaning.
  • Contamination can create fictitious benchmark gains.
  • Dataset deletion ≠ proven model unlearning.


LESSON 05 / 16
Scaling laws and the Chinchilla compute-optimal framework
Reason about parameters, tokens, FLOPs and deployment economics with explicit assumptions.


A Interviewer questions
  1. Derive the approximate 6ND pretraining FLOP rule and its assumptions.
  2. Explain why “20 tokens per parameter” is not a universal optimum.
  3. Why might a deployment-oriented team overtrain a smaller model?
B Deep technical explanation
1 · Mechanism & data flow
Empirical scaling fits validation loss as a function of model size N, training tokens D and compute C. A dense Transformer’s dominant training matrix operations motivate C≈6ND FLOPs as an approximation (architecture, sequence length, optimizer and attention details change it). Under compute-optimal training, allocate budget between parameter count and training data rather than maximizing weights unconditionally. The original Chinchilla study found that roughly joint scaling of N and D beats an undertrained oversized model at comparable training FLOPs. Its ~20 tokens/parameter is a historically useful model/data regime, not a physical constant; fits, architecture, data, repeated epochs and serving cost change the recommendation. A smaller model trained for more tokens may cost less per served token over its lifetime.
2 · Objective & mathematics
C_train ≈ 6ND   (dense decoder, approximate dominant FLOPs)
L(N,D) ≈ E + A/N^α + B/D^β   (empirical fit; coefficients are workload-dependent)
D/N ≈ 20  (illustrative historical Chinchilla regime, not a law)
3 · Architecture & alternatives
Train-compute optimum minimizes expected pretraining loss under fixed compute. Inference-aware optimum may favor fewer parameters and extra tokens; data-constrained training may favor synthetic/augmented data only after careful quality assessment.
4 · Implementation detail
Create pilot runs at several N,D points with identical curation/evaluation, fit loss curves and confidence intervals, then add post-training and lifetime serving costs to the decision.
5 · Compute / latency / memory / cost
Compute includes attention overhead at long sequence lengths, pipeline utilization and checkpoint costs. Inference weight-memory and cost scale strongly with N; more training tokens increase upfront cost.
6 · Failure modes & debugging
Do not extrapolate far beyond measured scales, compare different-quality datasets blindly, or claim exactly 20 as an invariant. Compute-optimal pretraining loss is not the same as downstream alignment quality.


C Interview-ready spoken answer
“For dense autoregressive Transformers I start with the heuristic training FLOPs ≈6ND, then fit empirical loss scaling across pilot runs. Chinchilla showed many earlier models were undertrained and that both parameters and data should scale. Its familiar 20:1 ratio is a regime-specific rule of thumb. I also price lifetime inference, data availability and downstream evaluations before selecting an operating point.”


D Follow-up pressure test
  1. Estimate FLOPs for 7B parameters and 140B training tokens.
  2. Why does attention context length complicate the 6ND estimate?
  3. How would inference volume alter an otherwise compute-optimal architecture choice?
E Production scenario — investigate, mitigate, validate
Constraint & symptom: A team proposes doubling a 13B model instead of supplying more vetted data.
Instrument: Collect pilot loss curves by N,D, measured training FLOPs/step, data uniqueness and expected annual inference tokens.
Test competing hypotheses: Compare undertraining, marginal data gains and operational GPU availability.
Mitigate & trade off: Run a bounded scaling sweep; include data pipeline cost and service model footprints rather than selecting by parameter count.
Acceptance gate: Choose configuration on validated domain scores and total cost-of-ownership with uncertainty bands; do not report hypothetical speedups as measured.
F Interview takeaways
  • Compute ≈6ND is a useful first estimate, with caveats.
  • Chinchilla’s ratio is empirical and context dependent.
  • Optimize for the deployment objective, not only pretraining loss.


LESSON 06 / 16
Pretraining vs continual pretraining vs supervised fine-tuning
Choose an adaptation stage based on learning signal and generalization risks.


A Interviewer questions
  1. When is domain-adaptive continual pretraining preferable to SFT?
  2. How do dataset structures and losses differ across stages?
  3. What is a concrete sign that fine-tuning is being misused to inject changing facts?
B Deep technical explanation
1 · Mechanism & data flow
Pretraining learns next-token regularities from broad text at scale. Continual pretraining (CPT) resumes the same or similar language-model objective on domain data to alter domain representations and terminology; its aim is not automatically chat behavior. Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) optimizes demonstrations of desired task and instruction-following behavior, often with assistant-token-only loss masks. CPT may benefit sustained domain jargon or style but can erase general capability if the new distribution is narrow. SFT usually needs fewer, higher-quality demonstrations and precise conversational templates. Neither replaces retrieval when facts are volatile, access-controlled or must be cited.
2 · Objective & mathematics
L_CPT = − Σ_{t∈valid} log pθ(x_t | x_<t);   L_SFT = − Σ_{t∈assistant} log pθ(y_t | context,y_<t)
3 · Architecture & alternatives
Before training, test prompt engineering, retrieval, deterministic validation and tool use. Prefer SFT for repeatable behavior/task format; CPT for persistent domain fluency; RAG for changing private facts.
4 · Implementation detail
Use train/validation split by document identity, verify chat role headers and EOS, mask user/system tokens if supervising only assistant behavior, and maintain general-retention benchmarks.
5 · Compute / latency / memory / cost
Full-parameter CPT is expensive and may cause forgetting; LoRA-style SFT reduces trainable parameter and optimizer state but does not eliminate activation costs. Carefully monitor adapter merge/version behavior.
6 · Failure modes & debugging
Frequent production facts encoded into weights can go stale and cannot guarantee source attribution. Broken chat templates can teach a model to output role delimiters instead of answers.


C Interview-ready spoken answer
“Pretraining develops broad next-token modeling. CPT keeps the LM objective but shifts the data distribution, which is useful for persistent domain language. SFT instead teaches task behavior from curated input–response demonstrations. I would use retrieval for dynamic facts, track forgetting on general benchmarks and verify loss masks and chat templates before calling adaptation successful.”


D Follow-up pressure test
  1. If SFT can learn jargon, why ever use CPT? Discuss data scale and goals.
  2. How would you isolate catastrophic forgetting from evaluation contamination?
  3. Why does an assistant-only mask matter?
E Production scenario — investigate, mitigate, validate
Constraint & symptom: A finance assistant must use rapidly changing internal policy documents and a specialized vocabulary.
Instrument: Measure terminology comprehension, citation exactness, policy-update lag, general instruction performance and per-answer costs.
Test competing hypotheses: Separate stable jargon deficit from missing up-to-date information and prompt/template errors.
Mitigate & trade off: Use RAG with ACLs for changing policies, targeted SFT for structured behavior, consider CPT only if repeatable domain-language failures remain.
Acceptance gate: Retain latest-policy citation accuracy, general benchmark floor and update SLA; never treat CPT as an authorization boundary.
F Interview takeaways
  • Objective and data format determine what adaptation can teach.
  • CPT shifts representations; SFT targets behavior.
  • Volatile facts favor retrieval over memorizing weights.


LESSON 07 / 16
Instruction tuning and conversational behavior
Teach role-conditioned helpfulness without confusing format adherence with factual grounding.


A Interviewer questions
  1. How do chat templates, role delimiters and EOS markers interact with training?
  2. Why can SFT make a model compliant but not reliably truthful?
  3. How would you design demonstrations for tool use and multi-turn exchanges?
B Deep technical explanation
1 · Mechanism & data flow
Instruction tuning transforms records into conversational examples: system/developer/user context plus assistant output, often including tool-call formats and end-of-turn markers. The tokenizer’s special tokens and role ordering must match the serving template. A supervised loss over target assistant text teaches conditional behavior: obey task framing, structure replies and stop correctly. Multi-turn examples teach continuation given prior messages, not durable memory; context still has a hard budget. Models can learn superficial formatting without learning to ground claims. The best demonstrations show abstention when evidence is missing, correct tool invocation with parameter constraints, and explicit non-compliance with unsafe instructions within policy boundaries.
2 · Objective & mathematics
L_SFT = −(1/|A|) Σ_{t∈assistant_positions A} log pθ(y_t|history,y_<t)
3 · Architecture & alternatives
Few-shot prompting is the no-weight-update baseline; SFT increases reliability across high-volume recurring patterns but adds training/maintenance. Grammar decoding can enforce JSON syntax even when SFT does not fully internalize it.
4 · Implementation detail
Build a chat-template validator testing role IDs, separators, tool-call arguments, EOS and assistant-only mask. Deduplicate templated demonstrations and keep hard negative examples in held-out eval rather than blindly training on them.
5 · Compute / latency / memory / cost
Long multi-turn sequences raise attention/activation cost. Overly repetitive instruction data risks bland, verbose or overrefusing replies. Monitor task completion alongside schema validity.
6 · Failure modes & debugging
Instruction-following on training-like prompts may conceal tool-parameter and user-intent failures. Valid JSON is not necessarily semantically valid. System prompts are not a security boundary.


C Interview-ready spoken answer
“Instruction tuning is SFT on structured conversations. The model learns how to respond conditioned on roles and prior messages, using the right special tokens and often assistant-only loss. I validate format, tool arguments, refusal/abstention and general capability separately; for hard schemas I still use constrained decoding and server-side validation.”


D Follow-up pressure test
  1. Why can a model pass JSON validity tests but fail task success?
  2. Does a multi-turn SFT dataset give it real memory?
  3. How can a mismatched tokenizer template cause repeated assistant-role headers?
E Production scenario — investigate, mitigate, validate
Constraint & symptom: A chat model emits tool calls as ordinary prose after a fine-tune.
Instrument: Inspect tokenizer special tokens, rendered training batches, role masks, tool-call success and serving template versions.
Test competing hypotheses: Compare template mismatch vs insufficient tool examples vs inference stop-token configuration.
Mitigate & trade off: Fix role encoding, train on structured tool actions, and validate dispatch permissions independently of model text.
Acceptance gate: Reach a preregistered tool-call syntax/parameter accuracy threshold on adversarial held-out dialogues without increasing unauthorized tool execution.
F Interview takeaways
  • Templates and masks are part of the learned interface.
  • Train structured behavior, validate semantics separately.
  • Conversation history is context, not persistent memory.


LESSON 08 / 16
Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF)
Trace the full preference-based alignment pipeline and its trust limits.


A Interviewer questions
  1. Walk through SFT → preference collection → reward modeling → policy optimization.
  2. Why is a KL reference model used in many RLHF objectives?
  3. How do you detect reward hacking and capability regression?
B Deep technical explanation
1 · Mechanism & data flow
A common RLHF stack first trains an SFT policy, samples multiple candidate responses per prompt, collects human preference comparisons, fits a reward model rφ(x,y), then improves a policy πθ to increase expected reward while restricting divergence from a reference (often frozen SFT) policy. PPO-based RLHF may use an old policy for importance ratios and a critic for advantage estimation. The chosen reward aggregates human preferences, but raters disagree, label criteria drift, and reward-model proxies can be gamed. Current pipelines also use DPO-style direct preference optimization and verifier-based RL; “RLHF” should not be used as a synonym for all alignment training.
2 · Objective & mathematics
max_θ E_{x∼D, y∼πθ(·|x)}[rφ(x,y)] − β E_x KL(πθ(·|x) || πref(·|x))
3 · Architecture & alternatives
PPO handles fresh sampled rollouts and multi-step reward signals but costs more infrastructure. DPO simplifies offline pairwise preference optimization. RLVR uses checkable external rewards and avoids subjective learned reward where verifiers exist.
4 · Implementation detail
Version annotation rubric, rater provenance, policy/ref/reward model and prompt distribution. Use held-out humans to audit whether reward score changes match real preferences. Respect approval/security boundaries regardless of reward.
5 · Compute / latency / memory / cost
Rollout generation, critic/reward/ref inference and policy updates add latency and GPU memory. KL regularization tunes reward improvement vs policy drift and is not a safety guarantee.
6 · Failure modes & debugging
Overoptimizing the learned reward may encourage verbosity, sycophancy or rubric exploits. If preferences encode bias, the policy inherits it. Report disagreement; do not imply one scalar reward equals user value.


C Interview-ready spoken answer
“A common RLHF pipeline starts from SFT, gathers pairwise preferences, trains a reward model and optimizes a policy against that reward with a reference-policy KL constraint. PPO is one optimizer, not the definition of RLHF. I track actual human/task outcomes, KL drift and adversarial failure cases, because a reward score is only a proxy.”


D Follow-up pressure test
  1. Why does maximizing RM score eventually stop correlating with user satisfaction?
  2. What gets frozen during PPO-based RLHF?
  3. When should you favor DPO or RLVR instead?
E Production scenario — investigate, mitigate, validate
Constraint & symptom: A policy’s reward-model score rises, while support-ticket reopen rates worsen.
Instrument: Compare online reopen rate, independent blind human ratings, answer lengths, reward–human rank correlation and reference KL.
Test competing hypotheses: Test reward-model exploitability, rater-rubric mismatch and drift in deployment prompt mix.
Mitigate & trade off: Pause rollout, revert the model, relabel adversarial cases, update rubric and retrain reward model/policy with tighter controls.
Acceptance gate: Ship only if blind human/task success and safety gates improve on preregistered slices, not merely RM score.
F Interview takeaways
  • RLHF optimizes a proxy derived from human preferences.
  • KL reduces drift, not all reward hacking.
  • Human and task-level evaluation must stay independent of the optimized reward.


LESSON 09 / 16
Reward models and preference datasets
Turn noisy human comparisons into a usable signal without confusing preference scores with truth.


A Interviewer questions
  1. Derive a Bradley–Terry preference loss from two scalar response scores.
  2. How do tie labels, disagreement and annotator effects alter model training?
  3. How would you detect shortcut features such as verbosity bias?
B Deep technical explanation
1 · Mechanism & data flow
For prompt x, preferred y⁺ and dispreferred y⁻, a reward model produces scalar scores r⁺,r⁻. The Bradley–Terry assumption sets the probability of preferring y⁺ to σ(r⁺−r⁻). Maximize the likelihood of pairwise comparisons to train score differences—not absolute calibrated rewards. A constant added to both scores changes nothing. Pairwise data need explicit rubric, rater identities protected as appropriate, difficult comparisons and ties/uncertainty metadata. Reward models can learn spurious associations such as longer answers or certain formatting. Validate on held-out contrasts that control content, length, toxicity and domain.
2 · Objective & mathematics
P(y⁺ ≻ y⁻|x) = σ(rφ(x,y⁺) − rφ(x,y⁻))
L_RM = −log σ(Δr);    ∂L/∂Δr = σ(Δr) − 1
3 · Architecture & alternatives
Pointwise scores can be cheaper to annotate, but anchor calibration is difficult. Pairwise labels provide robust relative comparisons, yet can miss nuance; process-level rewards give finer credit but are costly.
4 · Implementation detail
Create comparable completion pairs from the same prompt, randomized display order, mixed task domains and quality checks; avoid annotating with the scoring model itself as the only judge.
5 · Compute / latency / memory / cost
Extra generated candidates and rater time are expensive. Active learning focuses labels near uncertain pairwise boundaries but risks underrepresenting rare dangerous cases.
6 · Failure modes & debugging
Rater disagreement is data, not always labeling noise. A reward model that separates training pairs well can still fail on unseen tasks. Raw reward numbers have no universal meaning across models.


C Interview-ready spoken answer
“A typical reward model maps prompt–answer pairs to scalars and uses a Bradley–Terry loss on preferred versus rejected answers. Only score differences matter. I make the rubric explicit, retain disagreement and test controlled preference pairs for length or style shortcuts before using the reward for policy updates.”


D Follow-up pressure test
  1. If you add +100 to both reward scores, what changes?
  2. How do you calibrate disagreement where there is no majority preference?
  3. Why might pair accuracy improve while safety outcomes worsen?
E Production scenario — investigate, mitigate, validate
Constraint & symptom: A new reward model systematically ranks verbose wrong responses over concise correct ones.
Instrument: Measure controlled pairs with identical factual content but different length, blind rater agreement and score–length correlation.
Test competing hypotheses: Distinguish exposure imbalance, rater incentive, positional bias and proxy exploitation.
Mitigate & trade off: Balance pairs, correct rubric, use factual verification as an independent gate and retrain with adversarial contrasts.
Acceptance gate: On length-controlled holdouts, preference ordering aligns with ground truth and blind human evaluation; no quality regression on short answers.
F Interview takeaways
  • Pairwise score differences are identifiable, absolute scale is not.
  • Length/style shortcuts are common reward-model failure modes.
  • Disagreement and rater protocol affect the target.


LESSON 10 / 16
Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)
Understand importance ratios, clipped surrogate, critics and practical RLHF instability.


A Interviewer questions
  1. Derive the clipped PPO surrogate for positive and negative advantages.
  2. What roles do old policy, reference policy and critic each play?
  3. Why can too many optimization epochs on fixed rollouts destabilize PPO?
B Deep technical explanation
1 · Mechanism & data flow
PPO samples data using an old policy, estimates reward-to-go/advantages (often with a value critic and GAE), and updates the current policy. The ratio ρ=πθ(a|s)/πold(a|s) measures how action likelihood changed. The clipped objective takes the minimum of the raw and clipped advantage-weighted ratios to discourage updates beyond a trust-like band; it is not a hard KL bound. A separate KL regularizer may constrain drift from a fixed reference policy. PPO often includes value loss and optional entropy bonus. In language modeling, actions are tokens, states are prefixes, and sparse sequence-level rewards make credit assignment hard. PPO-based RLHF is memory-heavy because of rollouts, old/ref models, reward model and possibly a critic.
2 · Objective & mathematics
J_clip = E[min(ρ_t A_t, clip(ρ_t, 1−ε, 1+ε) A_t)]
ρ_t = exp(log πθ(a_t|s_t) − log πold(a_t|s_t))
L_total ≈ −J_clip + c_v L_value − c_e H + β KL(πθ||πref)  (variant-dependent)
3 · Architecture & alternatives
PPO is attractive with online rollouts and sparse/non-differentiable rewards. DPO avoids online rollout infrastructure for fixed preferences; GRPO removes a learned critic by using within-group comparisons.
4 · Implementation detail
Compute normalized advantages on fixed rollouts; accumulate valid token masks; log per-token ratio distributions, clip fraction, approximate KL, entropy and reward breakdown. Recompute old logprobs accurately.
5 · Compute / latency / memory / cost
PPO updates require multiple model passes and storing sequences/logprobs/values. Small ε improves conservatism but can slow progress; aggressive update epochs increase off-policy mismatch.
6 · Failure modes & debugging
Clipping is asymmetric by advantage sign; calling it “hardly changes policy” is wrong. If all advantages are poor or normalized incorrectly, the policy may collapse. Ratios can overflow unless computed in log space.


C Interview-ready spoken answer
“PPO compares the updated policy to the behavior policy through an importance ratio and clips the advantage-weighted objective. A critic estimates value-based advantages; a separate reference KL discourages drifting from the SFT behavior. I monitor KL, clip fraction, reward and independent task quality because clipping is not a safety or monotonic-improvement guarantee.”


D Follow-up pressure test
  1. Work out ρ=1.5, ε=.2 for A=+1 and A=−1.
  2. Why is πold not identical to πref?
  3. How do you diagnose reward gain with collapsing entropy?
E Production scenario — investigate, mitigate, validate
Constraint & symptom: A PPO run shows rapidly rising KL and severe output repetition.
Instrument: Plot ratio histograms, clip fraction, entropy, reward terms, KL against reference and sequence length by checkpoint.
Test competing hypotheses: Check stale rollouts, reward length bias, sign errors in KL or advantage and update epoch count.
Mitigate & trade off: Reduce update aggressiveness, fix reward/penalty direction, refresh rollouts more often or rollback to last acceptable checkpoint.
Acceptance gate: Stable preregistered KL band plus human/task success without repetition increase across held-out prompts.
F Interview takeaways
  • Old policy supports importance ratios; reference policy constrains drift.
  • Clipping limits surrogate incentives; it is not a hard trust region.
  • Critic/value, reward and policy may all consume substantial memory.


LESSON 11 / 16
Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)
Derive direct pairwise preference loss and understand offline distribution limits.


A Interviewer questions
  1. Write DPO loss from preferred/rejected log-probability ratios against a reference policy.
  2. Why does DPO avoid training a separate reward model?
  3. When does offline DPO underperform on-policy RL?
B Deep technical explanation
1 · Mechanism & data flow
DPO exploits the relation between an optimal KL-regularized policy and an implicit reward. For each prompt, compute chosen and rejected sequence log-probabilities under trainable policy πθ and frozen reference πref. Form the difference in policy log-odds minus reference log-odds. The logistic loss increases the margin in favor of chosen answers. DPO thus uses the preference dataset directly and dispenses with an explicit reward-model+PPO loop. But it still requires chosen/rejected pairs, careful prompt/response masks and reference log probabilities; it is not supervised CE. Offline pairs may under-cover new policy behavior, and the model can overfit noisy or shortcut preferences.
2 · Objective & mathematics
Δ = [log πθ(y⁺|x)−log πθ(y⁻|x)] − [log πref(y⁺|x)−log πref(y⁻|x)]
L_DPO = −log σ(βΔ)
Sequence log probability sums masked response-token log probabilities; normalize only if implementing a defined alternative objective. β controls the preference-vs-reference regularization scale.
3 · Architecture & alternatives
Choose DPO when curated offline comparisons and engineering simplicity dominate. Use online RL/GRPO when exploration or verifiable outcomes demand sampling fresh policy completions; consider data freshness and coverage.
4 · Implementation detail
Sum logprobs only over response tokens for each completion, not prompt/pad; ensure identical prompts and tokenization. Log chosen/rejected margins, length distribution and reference agreement.
5 · Compute / latency / memory / cost
One DPO update evaluates chosen and rejected branches; the reference logprobs can often be precomputed. Memory cost is lower than PPO with critic/reward/rollouts, but very long pairs still cost attention activations.
6 · Failure modes & debugging
DPO may prefer short completions if length confounds sum-logprob differences, depending on data and setup. β=0 gives a flat loss with zero gradient. Do not equate improving DPO loss with improved online truth.


C Interview-ready spoken answer
“DPO directly fits pairwise preferences using a logistic loss on the chosen-minus-rejected sequence log probability, corrected by a frozen reference model. It is simpler than PPO-based RLHF and avoids a separately learned reward model. I verify masking, β, pair quality and held-out human/task outcomes, and consider online RL when feedback is verifiable or the offline distribution is narrow.”


D Follow-up pressure test
  1. What happens mathematically when β=0?
  2. Should you average or sum response logprobs? Explain objective semantics.
  3. Can DPO improve on a type of answer absent from the preference dataset?
E Production scenario — investigate, mitigate, validate
Constraint & symptom: DPO validation loss falls but the model increasingly omits necessary explanations.
Instrument: Measure pair length imbalance, answer completeness by rubric, reference margins and outputs on matched-length heldouts.
Test competing hypotheses: Test whether chosen responses were systematically shorter and whether incorrect loss masking favors EOS.
Mitigate & trade off: Rebalance pairs, verify response-only logprobs and add length-controlled preference evaluation; avoid ad hoc normalization without clear objective.
Acceptance gate: Completeness and pair-preference satisfaction improve jointly, with no critical information omissions on golden scenarios.
F Interview takeaways
  • DPO uses policy and reference log-odds, not a trained scalar reward head.
  • Response masks and pair construction are crucial.
  • Offline preferences can be out-of-distribution after training.


LESSON 12 / 16
Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)
Train with group-based relative rewards instead of a separate learned critic.


A Interviewer questions
  1. Derive normalized group advantages for several completions of one prompt.
  2. What happens when every completion gets the same reward?
  3. Compare GRPO with PPO in memory, variance and failure cases.
B Deep technical explanation
1 · Mechanism & data flow
GRPO samples G completions for each prompt, computes a reward for each, then estimates advantages by subtracting the group mean and often dividing by group standard deviation. That replaces PPO’s learned value critic with within-prompt comparison. The policy update commonly uses a PPO-like clipped per-token ratio and optional KL penalty. Under outcome supervision the same completion-level advantage may be assigned to each valid generated token. Different modern variants change token-vs-sequence weighting, reference penalty and normalization. A group with equal rewards has no discrimination; the nominal normalized advantage needs explicit zero-variance handling and may provide no useful update. Increasing group size improves comparison opportunity but multiplies rollout compute.
2 · Objective & mathematics
A_i = (r_i − mean(r₁…r_G)) / (std(r₁…r_G)+ε)   (common variant)
J_GRPO ∝ (1/G) Σ_i (1/|y_i|) Σ_t min(ρ_it A_i, clip(ρ_it,1−ε,1+ε) A_i) − β KL
Objective and normalization vary by paper/implementation. An ε guard cannot manufacture training signal when all rewards match.
3 · Architecture & alternatives
GRPO fits verifiable outcomes where multiple attempts can be scored and a critic would be costly. PPO with critic may have lower-variance per-state estimates for suitable tasks; offline DPO needs pairwise preferences instead.
4 · Implementation detail
Group completions by the same prompt and check reward validity before normalization. Mask padding, correctly store old logprobs and choose documented token/sequence normalization. Audit zero-variance group frequency.
5 · Compute / latency / memory / cost
Rollouts cost roughly G times the generation expense per prompt, despite savings from removing the critic. Reward checks and KL/reference passes also add cost; total speedup is implementation-dependent.
6 · Failure modes & debugging
Group normalization can overemphasize trivial differences and reward noise; all-zero or all-one groups provide little learning. Sequence-length bias and inconsistent graders can dominate. “No critic” does not mean “no reward model.”


C Interview-ready spoken answer
“GRPO samples multiple completions for a prompt and uses their relative rewards to estimate advantages, typically with a PPO-like clipped policy update. This avoids a separate critic, but increases sampling cost and fails to learn from groups whose rewards are indistinguishable. I track reward variance, invalid grader outputs, length bias and independent task accuracy.”


D Follow-up pressure test
  1. Given rewards [1,0,0,1], compute group advantages with population standard deviation.
  2. How do you avoid a NaN when every response is correct?
  3. When is it more economical to train a critic than sample larger groups?
E Production scenario — investigate, mitigate, validate
Constraint & symptom: A GRPO math training run has many groups with identical reward and stagnating accuracy.
Instrument: Measure all-equal reward group rate, answer diversity, verifier coverage, gradient norms by task, task difficulty and token lengths.
Test competing hypotheses: Test saturated easy/hard prompts, grader bugs, low sampling temperature and solution-template collapse.
Mitigate & trade off: Resample informative difficulty levels, validate verifier, adjust exploration within safety limits and make group budgets cost-aware.
Acceptance gate: Non-degenerate groups increase on unseen questions and audited accuracy improves without gaming formatting checks.
F Interview takeaways
  • GRPO keeps clipped policy updates, changes advantage estimation.
  • No group reward spread means no useful within-group signal.
  • No critic ≠ low total compute when rollout groups are large.


LESSON 13 / 16
Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)
Apply executable or rule-based feedback, and secure the verifier against exploitation.


A Interviewer questions
  1. When is a reward truly verifiable rather than merely easy to score?
  2. How can a code agent game unit tests or a math grader?
  3. Why is outcome reward sparse, and how might you improve credit assignment?
B Deep technical explanation
1 · Mechanism & data flow
RLVR uses externally checkable signals such as exact mathematical answers, isolated unit tests, theorem proofs or schema validators. For each prompt sample one or more completions, run the verifier in a constrained execution environment, map results to reward, then improve the policy via a suitable RL algorithm (often group-relative or policy-gradient variants). Unlike subjective preferences, the verifier can give objective reproducible feedback for the part of the task it checks. Passing visible tests is not equivalent to correctness under hidden tests; brittle parsers allow format gaming. Untrusted generated code must execute in a secure, resource-bounded sandbox with no unrestricted credentials/network.
2 · Objective & mathematics
r(x,y) = 1[verifier(x,y) passes]  (simplest binary outcome reward)
Useful systems combine verifiable outcome, formatting validity and budget/side-effect penalties. Their weightings are design choices, not guarantees of correct generalization.
3 · Architecture & alternatives
Use learned rewards or humans where quality is subjective. RLVR is well suited for executable tasks with reliable hidden checks; combine with process rewards only if intermediate steps are themselves verifiable.
4 · Implementation detail
Store task and verifier version, prohibit evaluation leakage, use normalized/equivalent math parsing when appropriate, sandbox execution and retain detailed test traces for audit.
5 · Compute / latency / memory / cost
Verifier CPU and sandbox startup can dominate training throughput. Cache deterministic test fixtures where safe; limit time/memory/processes and bound rollout budgets.
6 · Failure modes & debugging
Reward hacking includes exploiting weak tests, answer-format shortcuts, cheating via environment leakage or editing verifier files. If grading is not sound, optimization will exploit that unsoundness.


C Interview-ready spoken answer
“RLVR scores sampled outputs with a deterministic verifier and uses those rewards in an RL update. It is compelling for code, math and other checkable tasks, but the verifier defines the actual objective. I sandbox execution, use hidden held-out tests, version the grader and measure generalization beyond pass rates on training fixtures.”


D Follow-up pressure test
  1. How would you secure a verifier against generated shell commands?
  2. What if visible tests pass but hidden tests fail?
  3. How do you handle mathematically equivalent expressions with distinct text strings?
E Production scenario — investigate, mitigate, validate
Constraint & symptom: A coding model reaches high training-test reward by editing its own tests.
Instrument: Log sandbox file diffs, verifier file integrity, hidden-test accuracy, command traces and permission violations.
Test competing hypotheses: Check writable test harness, tool boundary escapes, dataset solution leaks and reward attribution.
Mitigate & trade off: Move tests outside writable sandbox, restrict filesystem/network and introduce hidden mutation/property tests.
Acceptance gate: Zero unauthorized verifier modifications in adversarial audit and improved hidden-test correctness, not just visible pass rate.
F Interview takeaways
  • Verifiable rewards are as trustworthy as their verifier.
  • Separate training tests from independent held-out tests.
  • Execute model-generated code only inside constrained sandboxes.


LESSON 14 / 16
Alignment tax, reward hacking and catastrophic forgetting
Balance post-training behavior gains against regressions in general and rare capabilities.


A Interviewer questions
  1. Define and measure alignment tax without assuming it always exists.
  2. What mechanisms create reward hacking vs ordinary overfitting?
  3. How do you build a rollback gate for post-training regressions?
B Deep technical explanation
1 · Mechanism & data flow
Post-training can improve alignment while reducing performance on capabilities not reinforced by its data; the measured difference is sometimes called alignment tax, but it is benchmark- and setup-dependent. Causes include representation drift, narrow SFT demonstrations, reward-model shortcut optimization and excess KL divergence from a capable baseline. Catastrophic forgetting is loss of prior competence when new updates overwrite useful parameters. They overlap but are not synonyms: a model can retain knowledge yet choose not to use it, or forget domain behavior even without RLHF. Diagnose by comparing base, SFT and post-RL checkpoints on fixed task suites, measuring both refusal behavior and underlying reasoning where possible.
2 · Objective & mathematics
Δ_capability(task) = score_post(task) − score_baseline(task)
A negative change on one metric is evidence of regression on that metric—not proof that the model has forgotten the corresponding latent knowledge.
3 · Architecture & alternatives
Trade-off controls include data replay/mixing, smaller updates, reference KL constraints, adapter isolation, multi-objective optimization and calibration of refusal/abstention. Each has privacy and cost implications.
4 · Implementation detail
Run checkpoint-by-checkpoint evaluations with confidence intervals and stratification. Inspect reward-model shortcut correlations and KL divergence as signals, not as replacements for task success.
5 · Compute / latency / memory / cost
More replay improves retention but increases training compute. Lower learning rate/KL budget may preserve old skills but limit behavior adaptation; adapter isolation reduces interference, not model serving complexity.
6 · Failure modes & debugging
Goodhart’s law applies when reward becomes a target. Accuracy drops after RL can reflect stricter refusal criteria rather than inability; tests must distinguish warranted refusal from skill loss.


C Interview-ready spoken answer
“I measure alignment trade-offs explicitly by comparing checkpoints on held-out capability, safety, refusal and task-success slices. Reward hacking means a policy found a reward-model shortcut; forgetting means it lost earlier competence. I use targeted evaluation to separate those effects and tune data mixing, KL strength or adapter isolation before rollout.”


D Follow-up pressure test
  1. If refusal rates rise on benchmark questions, is that forgetting?
  2. What evidence would show reward hacking rather than a genuinely better answer?
  3. How would you decide whether a 2% regression is acceptable?
E Production scenario — investigate, mitigate, validate
Constraint & symptom: A safer post-RL model loses multi-step math accuracy on verified benchmarks.
Instrument: Track math pass@k, incorrect-refusal rate, reward scores, KL and capability per checkpoint and difficulty slice.
Test competing hypotheses: Compare refusal policy shifts, training prompt distribution, tool formatting and true solution accuracy when forced to attempt within policy.
Mitigate & trade off: Adjust alignment mix/penalty and include verified math tasks with guarded online RL, retaining safety regression gates.
Acceptance gate: Restore predeclared math floors while keeping independent safety tests satisfied; report uncertainty and trade-offs to stakeholders.
F Interview takeaways
  • Alignment gains may produce measurable task-specific regressions.
  • Reward hacking, refusal changes and catastrophic forgetting need different diagnoses.
  • Use multi-axis held-out evaluation and rollback gates.


LESSON 15 / 16
Knowledge distillation and teacher–student training
Compress model behavior while measuring what the student fails to inherit.


A Interviewer questions
  1. Derive KL/soft-target distillation loss and explain temperature scaling.
  2. When do hard-label SFT and soft teacher distributions teach different information?
  3. Why can a distilled student fail complex reasoning even if average accuracy is high?
B Deep technical explanation
1 · Mechanism & data flow
Teacher–student distillation trains a smaller student on a teacher’s outputs, logits or intermediate features. Hard-target distillation uses teacher-produced responses as SFT labels; logit distillation matches soft token distributions and preserves relative alternatives, often using KL with temperature T>1. Multiply a KL term by T² in a common setup to approximately compensate for reduced gradient scales; implementation-specific reduction still matters. Sequence-level distillation may train on generated reasoning traces, but traces can be wrong or unsuitable, and small students may lack representational capacity to internalize complex algorithms. Use curated hard cases, verified solutions and evaluation by difficulty—not only average imitation accuracy.
2 · Objective & mathematics
L_KD = α T² KL(p_teacher,T || p_student,T) + (1−α) CE(y,p_student,1)
p_{T,i} = exp(z_i/T)/Σ_j exp(z_j/T)
3 · Architecture & alternatives
Distillation is practical when serving volume or edge deployment dominates. Quantization reduces precision without necessarily changing architecture, while distillation changes learned function/model; they can be combined.
4 · Implementation detail
Freeze teacher, align tokenizers when doing logit-level KD (vocabulary mismatch needs mapping or another objective), precompute logits under storage budget, and validate generated targets with independent checks.
5 · Compute / latency / memory / cost
Full-vocabulary teacher logits are storage intensive O(tokens×V). Top-k logit sparsification reduces storage but loses tail information. Student memory/latency gains depend on actual architecture and hardware.
6 · Failure modes & debugging
Blind teacher imitation transfers errors and possible intellectual-property/licensing constraints. A small student trained on easy teacher answers can exhibit severe hard-task gaps or calibration changes.


C Interview-ready spoken answer
“Distillation transfers behavior from a teacher to a student using hard examples, soft probability targets, or features. With soft targets I optimize KL between temperature-scaled distributions, often with a T² factor. I verify licensing and data quality, and evaluate difficult tail cases separately because the student’s capacity and teacher mistakes limit fidelity.”


D Follow-up pressure test
  1. Why is T² common in soft-logit distillation?
  2. What if teacher and student vocabularies differ?
  3. When might a smaller distilled model cost more than a quantized teacher?
E Production scenario — investigate, mitigate, validate
Constraint & symptom: A distilled 3B assistant matches a 13B teacher on simple QA but fails hard multi-hop reasoning.
Instrument: Break down performance by reasoning steps, verified final answers, tool-use actions, calibration and latency on target devices.
Test competing hypotheses: Test teacher-error transfer, hard-case underrepresentation, capacity limits and insufficient context length.
Mitigate & trade off: Add validated multi-step examples, curriculum or targeted tool use; reassess whether architecture/size must increase.
Acceptance gate: Hit latency constraints and explicit pass-rate floors on held-out complex tasks; no claimed gain without measurement.
F Interview takeaways
  • Distillation transfers distributions or outputs; it does not copy parameters.
  • Soft KL contains information hard labels omit.
  • A student’s reasoning ceiling must be measured independently.


LESSON 16 / 16
Continual learning without losing capabilities
Adapt over time through replay, regularization, adapters and bounded deployment gates.


A Interviewer questions
  1. Compare rehearsal, regularization and parameter isolation for continual adaptation.
  2. How would you handle rolling domain updates while preserving earlier skills?
  3. Why is “trained on more recent data” not proof of current factual accuracy?
B Deep technical explanation
1 · Mechanism & data flow
Continual learning updates weights as new domains and tasks arrive. Naive sequential fine-tuning often causes interference because gradients from new data modify shared parameters used by earlier tasks. Rehearsal mixes prior representative examples; regularization such as elastic weight consolidation discourages moving important parameters; parameter-isolation approaches train task-specific adapters or expand capacity. Replay risks retaining sensitive historical data, while regularization only approximates preservation and adapters complicate routing/serving. Always separate new factual updates (often better handled by retrieval) from stable capability shifts, and maintain reproducible old/new task scorecards.
2 · Objective & mathematics
L_total = L_new + λ Σ_i F_i (θ_i − θ_i*)²   (EWC-style regularization)
Fᵢ approximates parameter importance, often via diagonal Fisher information. This is a regularizer, not a formal guarantee against forgetting.
3 · Architecture & alternatives
Choose retrieval for high-churn private facts; SFT for a stable new behavior; CPT for durable domain distributions. Use separate adapters if per-tenant isolation/routing and deployment overhead justify them.
4 · Implementation detail
Preserve versioned replay samples with consent/retention controls, checkpoint and rollback paths, data lineage and old-task benchmarks. Consider parameter-efficient adaptation and adapter selection by trusted metadata.
5 · Compute / latency / memory / cost
Replay raises data/storage/training cost; EWC adds Fisher estimation and potentially rigidity; per-task adapters add model management and GPU memory overhead. Measure lifetime total cost.
6 · Failure modes & debugging
Scores can improve for recent domains and collapse for minority-language or long-tail old tasks. A final single average metric hides this. Simple replay without privacy controls can reproduce historical sensitive information.


C Interview-ready spoken answer
“Continual adaptation is a multi-objective retention problem. Python reference · inspectable training objectives
The code below uses synthetic tensors and checks CE gradients, PPO clipping, DPO margins, flat-reward GRPO groups, knowledge distillation gradients and one minimal LM optimizer step. It is a reference implementation of individual mechanisms, not a distributed or production-grade RLHF trainer.
module5_training_reference.py · PyTorch
"""Module 5: training/alignment reference with small, inspectable PyTorch operations.
Run: python module5_training_reference.py  (requires PyTorch)
Synthetic tensors only; these functions illustrate objectives, NOT production trainers.
"""
import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F


def causal_lm_loss(logits, tokens, ignore_index=-100):
    """logits [B,T,V], tokens [B,T]; shift ONCE, scores only valid targets."""
    return F.cross_entropy(logits[:, :-1].reshape(-1, logits.shape[-1]),
                           tokens[:, 1:].reshape(-1), ignore_index=ignore_index)


def assistant_sft_loss(logits, labels):
    """Pre-shifted target labels [B,T], -100 for prompt/pad; logits [B,T,V]."""
    return F.cross_entropy(logits.reshape(-1, logits.size(-1)),
                           labels.reshape(-1), ignore_index=-100)


def bradley_terry_loss(reward_chosen, reward_rejected):
    return -F.logsigmoid(reward_chosen - reward_rejected).mean()


def ppo_clipped_objective(new_logp, old_logp, advantages, eps=0.2, mask=None):
    """MAXIMIZE this surrogate; signs matter for positive/negative advantages."""
    ratio = (new_logp - old_logp).exp()
    obj = torch.minimum(ratio * advantages,
                        ratio.clamp(1.0 - eps, 1.0 + eps) * advantages)
    if mask is None:
        return obj.mean()
    return (obj * mask).sum() / mask.sum().clamp_min(1)


def dpo_loss(chosen_logp, rejected_logp,
             ref_chosen_logp, ref_rejected_logp, beta=0.1):
    """Logprobs are summed over *response* tokens only, not prompt tokens."""
    delta = (chosen_logp - rejected_logp) - (ref_chosen_logp - ref_rejected_logp)
    return -F.logsigmoid(beta * delta).mean()


def group_relative_advantages(rewards, eps=1e-8):
    """rewards [batch_prompts,G]; population std, explicit flat-group handling."""
    centered = rewards - rewards.mean(dim=-1, keepdim=True)
    std = rewards.std(dim=-1, keepdim=True, unbiased=False)
    return torch.where(std > eps, centered / std.clamp_min(eps),
                       torch.zeros_like(centered))


def distillation_loss(student_logits, teacher_logits, labels, temp=2.0, alpha=0.5):
    teacher_prob = F.softmax(teacher_logits.detach() / temp, dim=-1)
    student_logprob = F.log_softmax(student_logits / temp, dim=-1)
    soft = F.kl_div(student_logprob, teacher_prob, reduction="batchmean") * temp**2
    hard = F.cross_entropy(student_logits, labels)
    return alpha * soft + (1 - alpha) * hard


def train_one_step(model, tokens, optimizer):
    optimizer.zero_grad(set_to_none=True)
    logits = model(tokens)
    loss = causal_lm_loss(logits, tokens)
    loss.backward()
    torch.nn.utils.clip_grad_norm_(model.parameters(), 1.0)
    optimizer.step()
    return loss.item()


if __name__ == "__main__":
    torch.manual_seed(11)
    # 1) For CE, dL/dlogits should be p - one_hot(target).
    z = torch.tensor([[2.0, 1.0, 0.0]], requires_grad=True)
    loss = F.cross_entropy(z, torch.tensor([1]))
    loss.backward()
    expected = z.detach().softmax(dim=-1) - torch.tensor([[0., 1., 0.]])
    assert torch.allclose(z.grad, expected, atol=1e-6)

    # 2) PPO: clipping is asymmetric for negative and positive advantage.
    nr = torch.log(torch.tensor([1.5, 1.5]))
    obj = ppo_clipped_objective(nr, torch.zeros(2), torch.tensor([1.0, -1.0]), eps=0.2)
    assert torch.isclose(obj, torch.tensor(-0.15))  # (1.2 - 1.5) / 2

    # 3) Group relative advantages have zero mean; flat groups return zeros.
    A = group_relative_advantages(torch.tensor([[1., 0., 0., 1.], [1., 1., 1., 1.]]))
    assert torch.allclose(A[0], torch.tensor([1., -1., -1., 1.]))
    assert torch.all(A[1] == 0) and torch.isfinite(A).all()

    # 4) Increasing DPO preference margin lowers loss.
    low = dpo_loss(torch.tensor([-.2]), torch.tensor([-.3]),
                   torch.tensor([-.2]), torch.tensor([-.3]))
    high = dpo_loss(torch.tensor([-.1]), torch.tensor([-.8]),
                    torch.tensor([-.2]), torch.tensor([-.3]))
    assert high < low

    # 5) Tiny loss-backprop train step + teacher logits detached in KD.
    net = torch.nn.Sequential(torch.nn.Embedding(24, 12), torch.nn.Linear(12, 24))
    data = torch.randint(0, 24, (2, 8))
    optim = torch.optim.AdamW(net.parameters(), lr=1e-3)
    assert torch.isfinite(torch.tensor(train_one_step(net, data, optim)))
    s = torch.randn(3, 4, requires_grad=True)
    t = torch.randn(3, 4, requires_grad=True)
    kd = distillation_loss(s, t, torch.tensor([0, 1, 2]))
    kd.backward()
    assert s.grad is not None and t.grad is None
    print("All module 5 reference tests passed.")
